# Changelog

All notable changes to **Site PWA** are documented in this file.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **The plugin's foundation** (#1). Namespace `SitePwa\` with an inline autoloader, PHP 8.3 and WordPress 6.8 floors, translations in `languages/` (pt_BR), and `uninstall.php` deleting everything the plugin stores — today only the update cache.
- **Updates from GitHub Releases** (#1). `Update\GithubUpdater` offers `releases/latest` on Dashboard → Updates, checks the package's SHA-256 before WordPress installs it, and fills "View details" from the offered release's readme, in Portuguese for a pt_BR administrator.
- **CI and releases the same as the other plugins** (#1). PHPUnit (unit on PHP 8.3/8.4, functional on the latest WordPress and on the floor), coverage floor, PHPStan, PHPCS, ESLint, Stylelint, Vitest, minified-asset check, Composer audit, CodeQL; a `v*` tag publishes the zip and its notes.
- **The app is installable** (#2). Settings → Site PWA turns it on (off by default) and sets its name, short name and colours. It lives under `/app/` with a manifest, the site icon or the plugin's, and an offline page; a service worker at the site root acts only on the app's pages and never caches a page.
- **Turning the app off cleans browsers up** (#2). While it is off, the worker URL serves a worker that deletes the app's caches and unregisters itself, so a phone that installed the app is cleaned on its next visit.
- **Another plugin's service worker keeps the app off** (#2). OneSignal, SuperPWA, PWA for WP or the PWA plugin, while active, keep the app off until the replacement is confirmed; the settings screen names them and lists the workers the browser already holds. A push service can share the root through `site_pwa_sw_imports` instead.
- **The app has its own frame** (#3). `/app/` and `/app/account/` need a signed-in user and draw a header, the screen and a bottom navigation, with landmarks, a skip link and `aria-current`; `wp_head()`/`wp_footer()` run, but the theme's stylesheets and scripts are taken out.
- **The login looks like the app inside the installed app** (#3). The WordPress login keeps its form and every plugin on it; styles apply only under `display-mode: standalone`, and the logo links back to the app when the login came from it.
- **Posts and pages open inside the app** at `/app/?p=ID` (#3), signed-in only, through the main query so shortcodes and their assets work. Canonical, Share, Copy link and Open on the site carry the permalink; a page-builder layout or page template opens on the site (`site_pwa_opens_in_app`).
- **The app can take its look from another plugin's design** (#3). Settings → Site PWA → Look picks the source of the accent colour and the corners; Free Form Certificate's Public Design is built in and others join through `site_pwa_identity_sources`. The accent applies to the light theme only, and a design switched off falls back to the theme colour.
- **Other plugins can add screens to the app** (#4) through the `site_pwa_register_screens` action, so a consumer needs no check that the app is installed. A screen is drawn in the frame at `/app/s/<id>/` or links to a page on the site, and the app checks its capability everywhere it appears. The bottom navigation is Home, up to three screens and More; More holds the rest and the account. Contract and example: `docs/screens.md`.
- **An invite to install, on phones only** (#16). The app's home screen shows the browser's own install button on Android, or the two steps on iPhone Safari. Visitors on the site's pages see a "Use the app" banner, signed in or not (#18), with settings for both. Neither shows on a computer or inside the installed app, and a dismissal lasts 30 days on that device.
- **News and pages inside the app** (#5). The home screen shows the three latest posts and the pages the administrator chose; More → News lists every post, ten to a page. Settings → Site PWA picks the news categories (none means all) and the pages, each of which can open on the site instead.
- **The app follows Free Form Certificate's Dark Mode** (#21). Settings → Site PWA → Light or dark picks light, dark, following the device, or FFC's Dark Mode, the default, so the app's header and menu match the FFC screens inside them; before, the frame followed the device and the screens their own setting.
- **The app's frame follows the approved mockup** (#23). Home opens on an accent band with the site's logo, a greeting, the name and a bell; then a dismissible banner, the next commitment, the pending items and the news. Other plugins feed it through the `site_pwa_home` filter, and a `nav` screen can take the raised centre slot of the bar (`'slot' => 'center'`). Inner screens lose the brand bar for a large title, with a back arrow on a post and the account. "Your screens" leaves Home, since the bar and More already list them. Contract: `docs/screens.md`.
- **More, News and the login follow the mockup** (#23). More opens on the person (initials, name, a line from `site_pwa_profile_line`, My details), groups screens into Services and Operator by the new `section` argument with a `subtitle` and a `badge` each, and ends with the site's pages, a dark theme kept on this device only, and Sign out. News offers its categories as chips (`?topic=`) and features the newest story. The login from the app names it, shows the tagline, keeps the lost-password link in the card and says "Keep me signed in on this device".

- **The app's icon is a setting** (#26). Settings → Site PWA → Icon picks the site icon (the plugin's when the site has none), the plugin's icon, or a square PNG of at least 512 px from the Media Library, refused with a message otherwise; the app makes the 192 and 512 px sizes from it, and the manifest, the home-screen icon and the login logo follow.

### Fixed

- **The app's screens have a title on a block theme** (#3). WordPress prints `<title>` only for a theme declaring `title-tag`, which a block theme does only inside its own template; the frame now prints it itself.
- **A light accent stays readable** (#26). Where the accent is text or an icon on a light surface (the bar's current item, links, Back, the Info tone, focus outlines, the login's link, the site banner's outline), a darker shade of the same colour reaching 4.5:1 is used; where it is a background it stays exact. The Info tone's background is now a tint of the accent instead of a fixed blue.
- **A changed colour or icon reaches an installed app** (#28). The manifest is served with `Cache-Control: no-cache`, so a host page cache cannot keep the old one; an installed app on Android takes the new colour of its system bar and its icon when the browser next refreshes it, which Settings → Site PWA now says.
- **The settings screen no longer warns about a service worker that never reaches the app** (#30). The browser check lists only a worker on the app's own scope or on a narrower one covering the app's pages, so Free Form Certificate's `/terminal/` worker is left out.
- **The bell always answers a tap** (#32). With nothing pending it was a plain icon and Home drew no Pending section, so a tap did nothing; it now always leads to Pending, which says "Nothing pending." when empty.
